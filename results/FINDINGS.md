## Findings on the v0.1 benchmark (synthetic data)

All numbers are on the 30 test sessions and are regenerated by `python run_benchmark.py && python report.py`.

1. **The literal procedures at the Appendix C defaults raise no alerts.** Recall 0.0%, 0 cases. The default imbalance multiple (k = 2x displayed depth within 3 ticks) is far above the size of every reference pattern, including the Chen pattern the specification cites.
2. **Section 6.1 read literally does not fix it.** Setting each account's threshold at the 99.5th percentile of its own entry imbalance gives recall 4.9%. The calibrated multiples came out between 0.227 and 15.5 (median 0.452), above nearly every spoof: a manipulator's own episodes are part of its baseline, and for other accounts the percentile is set by a few entries made when the book was thin.
3. **A fixed multiple trades recall against false alerts.** k = 0.05: recall 83.8% with 15.19 false cases per 100 legitimate account-sessions. k = 0.3: 0.0 false cases, recall 57.4%.
4. **Choosing k per account class from seeded baseline sessions (Sections 6.2 and 6.3) works better.** Recall 76.3% on faithful episodes and 40.3% on evasive ones, 5.33 cases per session, 0.93 false cases per 100 legitimate account-sessions. Chosen multiples: {'basis': 0.02, 'exec': 0.2, 'mm': 0.3, 'prop': 0.1}.
5. **Two patterns need procedure changes.** Without the flip extension, Oystacher-type recall is 1/489 (with it: 476/489), because the opposite-side execution follows the cancel instead of preceding it. Without the persistent-layering extension, Sarao-type recall is 0/48 (with it: 48/48), because those layers are not cancelled in response to a fill.
6. **Cross-instrument alignment is required for the Lebental pattern.** Without it: 0/130. With it: 46/130. Dropping the three-per-session repetition requirement raises it to 89/130, at the cost of 25.0 false cases per 100 legitimate account-sessions: a manipulator who acts a few times a session often does not meet the repetition rule.
7. **The exclusions matter.** Removing them raises false cases from 0.93 to 12.59 per 100 legitimate account-sessions.
8. **A ranking model trained on seeded labels is very good at patterns it has been shown and worse than the procedures at patterns it has not.** Average precision 0.995 against 0.658 for ranking by imbalance. Held out of training, its recall on Chen-type episodes is 43.0% (procedure: 55.0%), on Oystacher-type 36.4% (procedure: 97.3%). The near-perfect score on seen patterns is partly an artifact of regular synthetic episodes and should not be expected on real data.
9. **Detection falls off outside the documented patterns.** Chen-type recall by procedure drops to 2.8% once the cancel comes 5 to 10 seconds after the fill, and to 1.1% for spoof quantity of 15 to 30 lots.
10. **1,840 of 4,158 seeded episodes never got a fill** and are invisible to any fill-triggered procedure.
